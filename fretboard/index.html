 A GOOD PLACE TO START

Meet your next note.

  1. Choose a note family. Selecting C includes C3, C4, C5 and C6 across all 21 frets. Each octave is a distinct target. You can narrow strings, octaves and frets whenever needed.
  2. Press Play. Count four clicks, then follow the colored note through four bars of real 4/4 music. Quarter notes last one beat, half notes two, whole notes four.
  3. Read, then find. Turn note hints off to practice reading. A circled number is a classical guitar string instruction: ① is the thinnest string, ⑥ the thickest. The written pitch plus that string fixes the fret. Use Reveal positions when you need a nudge. “Wait for me” lets you take your time.
  4. Check your playing. Tap the fretboard or press Enter to self-report. Optional microphone mode checks the actual pitch, octave and onset timing. Play one clean note at a time and damp the previous note.
  5. Grow your palette. Raise the tempo as you get comfortable. Two clean passes at your target tempo mark this setup as ready for another note. Add one yourself, or enable automatic progression. This is a practice milestone for your current rhythm and range, not a guarantee of permanent mastery.
About C4 and the staff

All note names and frequencies here are the pitch you actually hear: C4 = 261.63 Hz. Guitar music is conventionally written an octave higher: sounding C4 appears as C5 on the treble staff. The small 8 under our clef tells you this. Different strings can produce the same exact pitch.

Microphone audio is processed locally and is never recorded or uploaded. While listening, app sounds are muted unless you enable headphones mode. Wear headphones to hear drums, the click and backing without feeding them into the microphone. The microphone can hear pitch, but cannot tell which string you used.

Choose a drum groove under Set the mood for recorded acoustic accompaniment. It follows the score tempo, with optional tom fills every four bars. Drums work in every key and can play alongside the metronome, generated bass, or your own file. Your uploaded file still plays at its original tempo. Preview groove plays four bars without starting a practice session.

The mechanical metronome uses recordings of a physical pendulum metronome, alternating its tick and tock. The first beat of each 4/4 bar can be accented. Metronome level is independent of drum level, under the master volume. Preview metronome plays four beats without starting practice.
